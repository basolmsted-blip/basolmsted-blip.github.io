Project aim

Named after the Greek goddess of memory, Mnemosyne explores the archaeology of memory: the ways in which material remains shape how the past is remembered, imagined, and understood. Rather than treating archaeology simply as the recovery of what happened, the project examines the processes through which fragments of the past are interpreted, reconstructed, displayed, contested, and incorporated into cultural memory.

At its heart, Mnemosyne asks how archaeology mediates our relationship with the past. Objects, monuments, landscapes, ruins, and archaeological narratives do not merely preserve history; they participate in its continual reconstruction. Through them, some histories are emphasized, others obscured, and meanings change across generations.

Mnemosyne therefore approaches archaeology not only as the study of past societies, but as the study of what happens to the past after it becomes memory.

Mission statement

Mnemosyne is an archaeological project concerned with the life of the past in the present.

It explores how archaeological remains become memory: how objects, monuments, landscapes, excavations, museums, scholarship, and popular imagination shape what societies believe about those who came before them.

Archaeology does more than recover the past. It helps create the past as we know it. Mnemosyne investigates that process.
